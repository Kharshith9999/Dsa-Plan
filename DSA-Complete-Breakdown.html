Suggested Order

Do not go tier-by-tier top to bottom. This ordering respects dependencies and front-loads interview yield:

1Tier 0 → 1 (arrays, binary search, sorting)3
2Tier 2 (hashing, string basics) + Tier 3 (LL, stack, queue)3
3Tier 5 (recursion/backtracking)2
4Tier 6.1–6.2 (trees, BST) + Tier 4 (heaps)3
5Tier 8.1–8.6 (DP core)5
6Tier 7.1–7.5 (graphs core)4
7Tier 9 (greedy) + Tier 10 (bits) + Tier 11.1–11.3 (math)3
8Tier 8.7–8.9 (advanced DP) + Tier 6.4 (segtree/BIT)4
9Tier 6.3, 7.6, 2.3 (LCA, SCC, KMP/Z)3

~33 weeks at a sane pace. You have roughly 16 months to December 2026, so this is comfortably achievable with room for two full revision passes.

Rules That Actually Determine Whether This Works

  1. Implement from scratch before solving problems with it. Reading a segment tree implementation and writing one are different skills, and only the second transfers.
  2. Spaced repetition, not linear progress. Every problem you failed goes on a re-solve list. Re-solve at +7 days and +30 days. If you can't re-solve it cold at 30 days, you never learned it — you recognized it.
  3. 20–40 minutes stuck, then read the editorial. Longer is romanticism. But after reading, close the tab and implement from memory. Never copy.
  4. Log every failure by cause, not by problem. Four buckets: didn't know the technique / knew it but didn't recognize it / recognized it but implementation bugs / complexity misjudged. After 100 problems the distribution tells you exactly what to fix. Most people assume bucket 1 and are actually in bucket 2.
  5. Pattern recognition is the actual skill. The bottleneck in an interview is never "I've never seen a monotonic stack." It's "I didn't notice this was a monotonic stack problem." That only comes from volume — roughly 400–600 problems for interview readiness, in the range of 1500+ for CF Expert.
  6. Contest regularly from now, not later. Codeforces Div. 3/Div. 2 weekly. Contest pressure is a different skill from untimed practice and it doesn't transfer automatically.
  7. Say the complexity out loud before you code. Interviewers weight this heavily, and it forces you to catch the wrong approach before you've spent 20 minutes on it.
